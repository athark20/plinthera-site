Our product · open source

AgentPermit Every agent. Explicitly permitted.

An enforcement and evidence gateway for AI agents. Before an agent issues a refund, grants access or releases a record, it asks AgentPermit. The answer is allow, ask a named person, or deny, and every answer comes with a signed receipt. The agent never reaches the system any other way.

  • Rules before actionsA catalogue of actions by domain and risk tier, with rules per agent: a refund over 1,000 waits for sign-off.
  • A named person signs offRisky actions pause until the accountable person approves them; nothing runs in the meantime.
  • Evidence, not just logsEvery decision produces a hash-chained, signed receipt that auditors can verify.
  • No way around itIdentity comes from the signed-in caller, and a paired data-loss policy stops agents reaching systems directly.

Built first for Microsoft Copilot Studio and Power Platform, with a custom connector, and callable from any agent runtime. Runs on Azure API Management, Entra ID, Azure SQL and Key Vault. .NET 10 gateway, 226 automated tests, Apache-2.0 licence.

Why the base

Seventy percent of what makes AI stick is not the model

It is the workflow the agent lands in, the data it can see, the rules it works under and the people who run it afterwards. We build those four things first, then the agents on top. Every engagement follows three rules.

Measured before, measured after

A baseline week is timed before any build starts. The saving we report is the difference, annualised, net of the time your team spends reviewing agent output.

Built on your tenant

Everything runs in your Azure, AWS or Google account, or your Claude Enterprise workspace, under your access controls. There is nothing to migrate at handover and no data-residency argument to lose.

Handed over, not held

Runbooks, training, admin access and an evaluation suite go to your team. The engagement ends when you can run the system without us; a retainer is a choice, not a dependency.

What clients ask before the first call

How long does it take

An Automation Sprint takes four to six weeks from signature to the first automations running with real users, after a two-to-three-week audit. Larger deployments run eight to sixteen weeks. We do not compress the baseline week; it is what makes the saving provable.

Claude Enterprise for organisation-wide rollouts, with Claude models through Anthropic's API, AWS Bedrock or Google Vertex AI for agents; Microsoft Copilot Studio, Azure AI Foundry, Azure AI Search and Power Platform for Microsoft estates; n8n or the platform's own tooling for orchestration. We sell the outcome, not the platform, and we build on the one you already pay for.

Nowhere new. Everything is built and run inside your own cloud tenant or, where required, on your premises. Plinthera staff access it through your identity provider with the permissions you grant and lose them at handover.

Fixed price per package with a written scope. Half on signature, the rest at the gates. Change requests are priced from a published rate card and start only when signed. Retainers are monthly in advance with a quarterly exit.

Thirty days of hypercare are included. After that your team runs the system with the runbook and evaluation suite we leave behind. If you want us to keep monitoring, upgrading models and reporting on governance, that is the Managed AI Operations retainer, and you can leave it at any quarter end.

AgentPermit is our open-source gateway that sits between AI agents and the systems they act on. Every action is checked against your rules before it runs: allowed, sent to a named person for sign-off, or denied, with a signed receipt for each decision. You do not need it to work with us; we recommend it wherever agents touch money, access or customer records. The core is free under the Apache-2.0 licence on GitHub, and we deploy and support it as part of an Agent and Copilot Deployment or Managed AI Operations.
